# Learning roadmap

## Curriculum shape

The first learning path has four connected tracks:

1. Mathematical foundations
2. Probability and statistics
3. Linear algebra
4. Calculus and optimization

The sequence is flexible. Foundation topics appear whenever they unlock a later concept, rather than becoming one long block that must be finished first.

## Topic map

### Mathematical foundations

- Arithmetic fluency, fractions, ratios, powers, roots, and logarithms
- Algebraic expressions, identities, rearranging equations, and inequalities
- Coordinate plane, graphs, slope, and interpreting functions
- Sets, notation, summation, and reading mathematical statements
- Pattern finding, counterexamples, estimation, and checking an answer
- Basic integration for continuous probability problems

### Probability and statistics

- Counting: permutations and combinations
- Probability axioms, sample spaces, and events
- Independence and mutual exclusivity
- Joint, marginal, and conditional probability
- Bayes theorem
- Mean, median, mode, standard deviation, correlation, and covariance
- Random variables; PMF, PDF, and CDF
- Expectation and variance, including conditional forms
- Uniform, Bernoulli, binomial, Poisson, exponential, normal, standard normal, t, and chi-squared distributions
- Central limit theorem
- Confidence intervals
- z, t, and chi-squared tests

### Linear algebra

- Vectors, vector spaces, and subspaces
- Linear dependence and independence
- Matrices and their operations
- Projection, orthogonal, idempotent, and partitioned matrices
- Systems of linear equations and Gaussian elimination
- Determinant, rank, and nullity
- Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
- Projections and quadratic forms
- LU decomposition
- Singular value decomposition

### Calculus and optimization

- Single-variable functions
- Limits
- Continuity
- Differentiability and derivative interpretation
- Taylor series
- Maxima and minima
- Single-variable optimization

## Two-week starter plan

Use weekdays only; weekends are optional catch-up or rest. The default is 90 minutes, adjustable using the session structure in the product plan.

| Day | Focus | Evidence to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Broad diagnostic and goal setup | Attempt 12 questions: four from probability, linear algebra, and calculus. Record confidence, unknowns, and foundation gaps. |
| 2 | Foundations: equations, notation, and functions | Complete a short skills check and explain two transformations in words. |
| 3 | Matrix operations and small linear systems | Solve 4–8 questions and explain one elimination step without notes. |
| 4 | Elimination, rank, and consistency | Compare unique, absent, and multiple-solution cases. |
| 5 | Vectors, span, and independence | Give an example and non-example; repair the week's most important error. |
| 6 | Counting, sample spaces, and probability rules | Build sample spaces and distinguish event relationships. |
| 7 | Conditional probability and Bayes | Represent problems with a table or tree before calculating. |
| 8 | Discrete random variables, expectation, and variance | Create a valid PMF and compute its main quantities. |
| 9 | Functions, limits, graphs, and derivative meaning | Check calculus foundations and record specific gaps. |
| 10 | Mixed checkpoint and replanning | Use fresh questions, compare confidence with accuracy, and choose the next priority. |

This is a foundation and diagnosis phase, not an attempt to finish every topic in two weeks. If the baseline reveals serious algebra gaps, Days 2–4 become targeted foundation work and the remaining sequence shifts forward. A topic that appears strong still needs fresh independent questions before it is deprioritized.

## Twelve-week first pass

This sequence includes the starter fortnight. At 5–10 hours per week, it represents roughly 60–120 hours. Actual pace will depend on the diagnostic and review load.

| Weeks | Emphasis | Checkpoint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | Diagnostic, learning routine, foundations, and first concepts | Initial topic profile, repair queue, and sustainable weekday habit. |
| 3–4 | Linear algebra | Mixed questions across systems, spaces, matrix properties, rank, eigenpairs, projections, quadratic forms, LU, and SVD. |
| 5–6 | Probability models | Joint and conditional probability, random variables, PMF/PDF/CDF, expectation, and distribution families. |
| 7–8 | Statistics | Descriptive quantities, covariance/correlation, central limit theorem, confidence intervals, and hypothesis tests. |
| 9–10 | Calculus and optimization | Limits, continuity, derivatives, Taylor approximation, extrema, and optimization. |
| 11–12 | Consolidation | Timed mixed sets, delayed mistake repairs, recall, and remaining weak areas. |

Reserve 15–25% of every week for earlier topics. The learner may reorder blocks or split them when a prerequisite needs attention.

## Longer-term direction

After the first pass, repeat a cycle of mixed practice, weak-area repair, and timed problem sets. The platform should generate each cycle from evidence instead of imposing the same calendar on every learner.

The broad progression is:

1. Build meaning and vocabulary
2. Solve routine untimed questions
3. Mix nearby concepts so the method is not obvious
4. Revisit after a delay
5. Add time pressure gradually
6. Explain recurring errors and repair prerequisites

The long-term learning target is mathematical judgment: recognizing structure, choosing a method, checking an answer, and explaining why it works.
